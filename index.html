03 / 10 — Methodology

Empirical &
Descriptive Design

Structured questionnaires were administered using convenience sampling. The questionnaire incorporated a five-point Likert scale to measure attitudes and perceptions concerning online reviews.

Secondary sources included peer-reviewed journals, government reports and statistical databases. The study examined demographic and behavioural variables including age, gender, educational qualification, occupation, purchasing frequency and review-platform usage.

04 / 10 — Key Areas Examined

What the Study Examined

  • 01 The factors that have the greatest influence on consumers' evaluation of online reviews.
  • 02 Whether online reviews help consumers make informed purchase decisions.
  • 03 Whether consumers consider online reviews an important source of product information.
  • 04 Whether consumers normally read online reviews before purchasing a product.
  • 05 How consumers perceive the credibility of online reviews.
  • 06 The level of trust consumers place in online reviews while making a purchase.
05 / 10 — Results

Greatest Influence on Review Evaluation

The research paper reports the strongest observed subgroup patterns for factors influencing consumers' evaluation of online reviews.

Factors Influencing Evaluation of Online Reviews
Result & discussion: The reported patterns show that different consumer groups respond to different review cues. Verified-buyer reviews were particularly prominent for respondents aged 46–50, while customer photos/videos were especially prominent among female respondents.
06 / 10 — Results

Online Reviews and Informed Decisions

The paper reports strong subgroup patterns in responses to whether online reviews help consumers make informed purchase decisions.

Reviews Help Consumers Make Informed Purchase Decisions
Result & discussion: The results indicate a generally positive perception among several groups, particularly female, PG and weekly online purchasers. At the same time, some groups reported neutrality, showing that the usefulness of reviews is not perceived uniformly across all demographic categories.
07 / 10 — Results

Reading Reviews Before Purchasing

The study examined whether consumers normally read online reviews before purchasing a product.

Consumers Reading Reviews Before Purchasing
Result & discussion: Reading reviews before purchasing appears to be a prominent behaviour in several reported subgroups. Female respondents showed 94% agreement, while the reported strongest patterns for age 46–50, PG respondents and monthly purchasers reached 100%.
08 / 10 — Results

Perceived Credibility of Online Reviews

Respondents were also asked how they generally perceive the credibility of online reviews.

Perceived Credibility of Online Reviews
Result & discussion: The reported findings show variation in perceived credibility. Female respondents showed a strong high-credibility response, while private-employed, weekly-purchasing and Google Reviews groups showed reported 100% high-credibility patterns. The 46–50 age group was reported as moderate rather than high.
09 / 10 — Results

Trust in Online Reviews During Purchase

The final result theme considers the level of trust respondents place in online reviews while making a purchase.

Trust in Online Reviews While Purchasing
Result & discussion: Trust is more nuanced than simply reading reviews. Weekly purchasers showed the strongest reported trust pattern, while female and yet-to-be-employed groups showed a "trust a little" pattern. The PG subgroup had a contrasting reported pattern of "do not trust at all", demonstrating variation across educational groups.
10 / 10 — Conclusion

Results & Discussion

Overall finding: The empirical results show that online reviews play a meaningful role in consumer purchase-related behaviour, but the way consumers evaluate and trust reviews varies across demographic and behavioural groups.

Review evaluation

Verified-buyer reviews, customer photos/videos, written reviews and star ratings each emerged as important cues for different respondent groups.

Purchase behaviour

Several subgroups reported agreement that online reviews help with informed decisions and that they normally read reviews before purchasing.

Credibility

Perceived credibility varied considerably between groups, ranging from moderate to high in the reported subgroup patterns.

Trust

Trust was not uniform. Some groups reported trusting reviews, while others showed neutral, limited-trust or no-trust patterns.

Research implication: Online reviews should therefore be understood as a multi-dimensional source of consumer information rather than as a single star-rating mechanism. The findings also need to be interpreted in light of the study's convenience sampling, geographic scope and self-report limitations.
