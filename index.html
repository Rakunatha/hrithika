An empirical study examining how consumers evaluate, use and trust online reviews while making purchase decisions.

Study in Numbers

The research reports an empirical study based on 224 respondents from diverse demographic backgrounds. The study examines awareness and attitudes towards online reviews across demographic and behavioural variables.

Research Methodology

The study uses a structured questionnaire and empirical analysis. The questionnaire examines demographic characteristics together with respondents' perceptions and behaviour concerning online reviews.

What the Results Examine

The results examine factors influencing evaluation of online reviews, whether reviews help consumers make informed purchase decisions, their importance as a source of product information, review-reading behaviour before purchases, perceived credibility and consumer trust.

Conclusion

Overall, the paper concludes that there are variations in awareness and attitudes towards online reviews across demographic groups. The findings identify differences in how consumers evaluate, use and trust online reviews when making purchase decisions.

The paper also recognises limitations relating to sample size and geographic coverage, which should be considered when interpreting the findings.
